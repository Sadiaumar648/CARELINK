1 · Overview

CareLink digitises a real, currently operating caregiving agency that places babysitters and nannies with families in Ghana. Today the agency runs entirely on phone calls and in-person conversations: no online presence, no structured record of bookings or payments, no reviewable record of who has been verified and how. The agency's staff are its product, and it has no catalogue of them.

CareLink is not a marketplace open to any agency. It is the operating system of this agency: the administrator manages the roster, verification and disputes; staff maintain their own profiles and availability and answer booking requests; parents discover, book, pay for and review that staff. Everything the agency currently holds in memory or in a notebook becomes queryable data — who is available this Saturday in Adenta, which staff are verified, what has been earned this month, and which arrangements are in dispute.

1.1 Vision statement

A parent should be able to find, verify, book and pay a trusted caregiver from the agency in under five minutes — and never have to explain their child's routine twice. The agency should be able to see its whole operation on one screen.

1.2 What the system tracks

The agency's operations translate into four things the platform must account for continuously. These four are the backbone of the data model and of the administrator's dashboard.

TrackedWhat it means at CareLinkWhy it matters
Caregiver supplyThe roster: every staff member, their verification status, skills, service areas, hourly rate and declared availability windowsThe agency's only sellable capacity. Unverified or unavailable staff cannot be booked, so supply must be accurate to the hour
BookingsEvery request, acceptance, decline, cancellation, active session and completion, with locked-in termsReplaces verbal arrangements; the single source of truth in a dispute
RevenueMobile Money payments against bookings: gross booking value, agency commission, staff payout, refundsThe agency has no financial record today; this is the first one

2.1 Non-goals

Explicitly not in this phase: onboarding other agencies, nurse and home-healthcare placements, native mobile apps, certified biometric KYC, live criminal-record or background-check integrations, real money movement through a Mobile Money aggregator, GPS tracking during a session, and multi-language interfaces. Each is recorded as future scope, not as an omission.

Role-based access control is absolute: each role sees only its own interfaces and only the records tied to it. A parent never sees another parent's household; a staff member sees a Ward Care Profile only for a booking they have accepted.

4 · Feature requirements

Each feature carries a priority — P0 the system is not usable without it, P1 required for the evaluated MVP, P2 desirable if time allows. Acceptance criteria in full are in the Engineering Plan; the criteria named here are the ones that define the feature.

F1 · Accounts and role-based access · P0

Email-and-password registration and sign-in for parents and staff; administrator accounts are provisioned, not self-registered. Every account carries exactly one role, set at creation and not user-editable. Sessions persist across reloads; sign-out clears them. Every protected route and every API endpoint checks role and record ownership on the server, not just in the interface.

Defining criteria. A staff account requesting a parent-only route is refused by the server. A parent requesting another parent's booking record receives a not-found, not a redirect.

F2 · Roster management · P0

The administrator can add a staff record directly — name, phone, area, indicative rate — before that person has ever logged in, mirroring how the agency already holds its people. Adding a record issues an invitation and a first-login credential. The staff member then completes their own profile and verification. The roster view lists every staff member with verification status, availability summary, rating, bookings completed and earnings to date, and can be filtered by status.

Defining criteria. A roster record created by the administrator and never claimed appears as invited and cannot be booked. Claiming the invitation preserves the same record rather than creating a duplicate.

F3 · Bidirectional identity verification · P0

Both parents and staff verify themselves — not staff only. A submission carries: government ID type and number (Ghana Card, with passport or voter ID as fallback), a photograph of the document, a confirmed phone number, and at least one contactable reference with name, relationship and phone. Status moves unverified → pending review → verified or rejected, with a reason recorded on rejection and resubmission allowed.

Mandatory for verified status: an ID matching the profile name and showing the holder is 18 or over, a confirmed phone number, one contactable reference, and no unresolved report against the account. Optional supporting evidence: the live photo check below, and for staff, certification uploads such as First Aid/CPR or an Early Childhood Education certificate.

Defining criteria. Unverified staff never appear in search results. An unverified parent can browse but cannot submit a booking request.

F4 · Live photo plausibility check · P1

An optional step within verification: the user captures a live selfie in the browser, which is compared by a vision model against the uploaded ID photograph. The model returns one of three plausibility results — likely match, uncertain, likely mismatch — with a one-line explanation, shown to the administrator alongside both images. It is a second opinion for a human reviewer, never an automated decision.

Defining criteria. The step can be skipped without blocking verification. The user is told, before the camera opens, that the photograph is sent to a third-party service and that the result is not a certified biometric match. The administrator's decision is recorded independently of the model's result, so the two can be compared during evaluation.

F5 · Staff profiles and availability · P0

A staff profile carries a photograph, bio, years of experience, hourly rate, service areas, emergency contact, certifications and skill tags drawn from a fixed vocabulary: infant care, toddler care, school-age supervision, newborn care, meal preparation, special-needs care, light housekeeping, and local languages spoken. Availability is declared as recurring weekly windows plus specific unavailable dates. Rate and availability changes never alter the terms of an already-accepted booking.

Defining criteria. Declared availability drives both search filtering and the availability component of the match score. A staff member cannot hold two accepted bookings that overlap in time.

F6 · Search, filters and weighted matching · P0

Parents search by area, date and time, budget, minimum experience, minimum rating and skill tags. Results are not a flat filtered list: every candidate is scored and ranked, and every card shows why it ranked where it did — a visible breakdown of the contributing factors.

FactorWeightHow it scores
Availability fit30%Full score when the requested window falls inside declared availability with no conflicting booking; partial when only part of the window is covered
Area proximity25%Full score for the parent's own area, reduced for a declared neighbouring area, zero outside the service areas
Skill match20%Proportion of the requested skill tags the staff member holds
Rating and experience15%Average review rating and years of experience, normalised; staff with fewer than three reviews score at the roster median rather than zero
Budget fit10%Full score at or under the stated budget, tapering above it rather than excluding outright

Weights live in configuration, not in code branches, so the agency can retune them and the evaluation can compare rankings. Defining criteria. Two parents with the same query get the same ranking; the breakdown shown to the parent sums to the score used for ordering.

F7 · Natural-language search · P1

A parent may instead type or dictate a request in plain language — "someone in East Legon for Saturday afternoon who can handle a newborn, under 30 cedis an hour" — which a language model converts into the same structured filters. The interpreted filters are shown as editable chips before the search runs, so the parent can correct a misreading. If parsing fails or returns anything unexpected, the interface falls back to the manual filters with a plain explanation and no error state.

Defining criteria. The model's output is validated against a strict filter schema before it touches the query. Nothing beyond the recognised filter fields ever reaches the search.

F8 · Ward Care Profile · P0

A parent creates one profile per child, once: name or nickname, age, allergies and medications with dosage and timing, meal times and food notes, nap, homework and play schedule, special needs, house rules and general notes. It attaches automatically to every booking for that child and is visible to the assigned staff member from acceptance until the booking is closed — never to anyone else, and never browsable from a staff profile.

Defining criteria. A parent booking a second time re-enters nothing. Editing the profile updates what staff see on future and currently active bookings, with the edit timestamped so a caregiver can see it changed.

F9 · Booking lifecycle · P0

A booking moves through pending → accepted → paid → active → completed, with declined, cancelled and disputed as terminal or parallel states. The parent submits date, start and end time, address, the child and any one-off notes; the caregiver sees the request with the parent's verification badge and Ward Care Profile and accepts or declines with a reason. On acceptance the terms — date, hours, hourly rate, total, address — lock: neither side can silently change them, and any change requires a cancellation and a new request.

On the day, either party marks the session active; either marks it complete. Only while a session is active do the two sides see each other's emergency contact, symmetrically and temporarily.

Defining criteria. Every state change is timestamped and attributed. A caregiver cannot accept a request that overlaps an accepted booking. An expired pending request lapses rather than sitting open indefinitely.

F10 · Mobile Money payment · P1

After acceptance the parent pays via a Mobile Money flow covering MTN, Telecel and AirtelTigo: choose network, confirm the phone number, confirm the amount, receive a payment reference. The reference and status attach to the booking and are visible to both sides and to the administrator. The flow is simulated in this phase — a sandbox that models initiation, pending, success, failure and timeout — behind an interface shaped so a real aggregator can be substituted without touching the booking code. The recorded payment splits into agency commission and staff payout at a configurable rate.

Defining criteria. A booking cannot enter active without a successful payment or an explicit administrator override recorded as such. A failed payment leaves the booking accepted and retryable, never half-paid.

F11 · Messaging · P1

Text messaging scoped to a booking, opening on acceptance and closing when the booking is completed or cancelled. Messages are timestamped, ordered, marked read, and retained as part of the booking record so they are available to the administrator if a report is filed.

F12 · Reviews and ratings · P1

After completion the parent leaves a one-to-five rating and a comment. The caregiver's public average and review count recalculate immediately. One review per booking; reviews are attributable to a completed booking, so they cannot be manufactured. A caregiver may respond once to a review. The administrator can hide a review that breaches conduct rules, with a reason recorded.

F13 · Reports and dispute resolution · P0

Either party can file a report against a booking at any point — no-show, conduct, payment, safety, other — with a description. Reports route to the administrator, never to the counterparty, and carry a status of open, investigating or resolved with a resolution note. An account with an unresolved report cannot reach verified status.

F14 · Administrator dashboard and operational reporting · P0

One screen for the agency's whole operation: bookings by status and by period; revenue with gross booking value, commission earned and staff payouts; roster health showing verified, pending and invited counts and the utilisation of each staff member; the verification queue with age of oldest pending item; and the open report queue. Each figure is filterable by date range and exportable as CSV, because the agency will want to reconcile against its own records.

Defining criteria. Every dashboard figure is derived from booking, payment and verification records — never entered by hand — so the dashboard cannot disagree with the underlying data.

F15 · Accessibility for low-literacy users · P1

Every step of staff onboarding, verification and booking response supports voice input for text fields and read-aloud for instructions and incoming requests, using the browser's speech APIs. Beyond speech: icon-and-photograph reinforcement of key actions, a plain-language register throughout, and no step that depends on reading a paragraph to complete correctly. Where speech APIs are unavailable, the interface degrades to text without losing function.

F16 · Notifications · P2

In-app notifications for the events that require a response: a new request, an acceptance or decline, a payment confirmation, a session start, a completed booking awaiting review, and a verification decision. Email or SMS delivery is future scope.

6 · Assumptions, constraints and dependencies

Assumptions. Users have a smartphone or computer with internet access, and a working camera where they choose live photo verification. Parents and staff will supply accurate information. Electricity and connectivity are workable if intermittent. Users keep their own credentials safe.

Constraints. A compressed August–September 2026 build window; simulated Mobile Money and mock verification data; AI accuracy that must be measured rather than assumed; and testing in a controlled environment with recruited participants rather than a public launch.

Dependencies. Supabase for database, authentication and file storage; a hosted language-model API for search parsing and the photo plausibility check; the browser's camera and speech APIs; Vercel for deployment. Each is isolated behind an internal interface so a failure degrades one feature rather than the product.

8 · Open questions

  1. Does the agency take its commission from the parent's payment or invoice staff separately? The dashboard's revenue figures depend on the answer.
  2. What is the cancellation policy — how late may a parent cancel, and is any portion retained?
  3. Should a caregiver be able to propose an alternative time instead of only accepting or declining?
  4. How long should identity documents and selfies be retained after a verification decision, and who at the agency may re-open them?
  5. Do parents need verification before browsing, or only before booking? The current specification allows browsing.
