CareLink Design Brief

The look, voice and interface vocabulary of the platform — a fixed palette, one typeface, a small set of components, and a stated intent for every key screen, so nothing is decided twice or decided at random.

1 · Design intent

CareLink is a document, not an app-store product. A parent is choosing who will be alone with their child; a caregiver is presenting their working life. Both deserve an interface that reads as a record — calm, legible, printed-looking — rather than a bright marketplace competing for a tap.

Three consequences follow. Text carries the hierarchy, not boxes: size, weight and space organise a screen, and cards are used only for genuinely discrete items such as a caregiver in a result list. Colour is spot colour: one interactive blue, one rare magenta for alerts and warnings, and nothing else — a screen with colour everywhere has no emphasis. Nothing decorative: no illustration, no gradient, no shadow that is not doing the job of lifting a sheet above the page.

Adjectives to design against: plain, calm, verified, unhurried, unembarrassed by text. Adjectives to design away from: playful, gamified, glossy, crowded.

2 · Colour

A paper ground with near-black text and two process accents. The blue is the only interactive colour: if something is blue, it can be pressed. Magenta is reserved and rare — destructive confirmation, an unresolved report, a rejected verification — so that seeing it means something.

Rules: never both accents in one small component; never colour as the only carrier of meaning — a status is always a word as well as a tint; and every text-and-background pair meets WCAG AA, which is why paragraph-sized accent text uses the deep cyan step.

3 · Typography

One family throughout: Source Serif 4, at 400 for body, 600 for headings, with the true italic for emphasis and quoted care instructions. A serif interface reads as a record rather than a feed, and it holds the long instruction text that low-literacy support and care profiles both require. No second family — the serif is the interface chrome too.

Never below 13px anywhere; never below 15px for anything a caregiver must read to do their job. Measure caps at 66 characters. Amounts and times use tabular figures so columns align. Currency is always written GH₵ with the amount, never a bare number.

4 · Layout, spacing and motion

A four-step spacing scale — 10, 15, 20, 30, 40px — and nothing between. Corner radius is 2px, effectively square: this system does not round its corners. Content is flush left with air on the right; screens are single-column on mobile and a maximum of two columns on desktop, never three.

Sections are separated by space, not by rules or boxes. The only elevation in the system is the sheet that lifts a modal or a bottom sheet above the page, and there is exactly one shadow step for it. Motion is functional and brief: 150ms for a state change, 250ms for a sheet entering, no easing that overshoots, and no animation on data appearing — a list does not perform.

Touch targets are 44px minimum, and 52px on every caregiver-facing screen, where the primary action is full-width and unmissable.

6 · Imagery and icons

Only three kinds of image exist: caregiver profile photographs, identity documents in the verification review, and the landing page's single photograph. Photographs of children are never collected or displayed. Profile photographs are square with a 2px radius, shown at a consistent size, and never cropped to a circle. The landing photograph takes the system's print treatment — a halftone dot screen — so it reads as printed rather than stock.

Icons are Phosphor in the duotone weight, at 20px inline and 24px in navigation, and always accompanied by a word in caregiver-facing navigation. No icon carries meaning alone.

7 · Voice and copy

Plain, short, second person. Say what a button does — Send request, Pay GH₵120 with Mobile Money — never Submit or Continue where something more specific is true. Errors name the cause and the fix and never blame the user. Nothing in the caregiver flow requires reading a paragraph to complete correctly. And the platform never overstates its own certainty: the photo check is described as a plausibility aid for the agency's reviewer, in those terms, wherever it appears.

9 · Accessibility commitments

WCAG AA contrast on every pair. Full keyboard operation with a visible focus ring. Every input labelled, every error associated with its field. Voice input and read-aloud on every caregiver-facing screen, degrading to text where the browser lacks support. Status never carried by colour alone. Usable at 320px and at 200% text zoom without loss of function.

10 · What this system does not do

No dark surfaces. No gradients. No decorative illustration. No emoji. No card used to fence off a section of a page. No second typeface. No third colour. No animated data. No skeleton shimmer where a plain "loading" line will do. Each of these is cheap to add and expensive to remove, and none of them makes a parent more confident about who is coming to the house.
